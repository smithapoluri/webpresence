My Belief

The unpopular
take on AI programs

Most AI programs fail not because the models aren't good enough — they fail because the program infrastructure around the model isn't designed to hold. Ambiguous ownership, unmeasured risk tolerance, and the misalignment between what applied science optimizes for and what compliance needs are what sink most initiatives.

My approach: Build the shared definition of "done" before the first sprint. Engineer compliance in — not on. Make risk visible from day one. The programs that survive are the ones where the process is as rigorously designed as the product.

Multi-region Compliance Engineering

The Challenge

Operating ML-powered systems across global marketplaces means navigating a complex web of regional regulatory requirements while keeping systems resilient and automation-first. Manual compliance workflows simply don't scale at Amazon's pace — they had to be engineered out of the operating model.

My Approach

  • Mapped compliance requirements across regions and built unified frameworks satisfying multiple regulatory contexts
  • Embedded automation into compliance workflows to eliminate manual toil and reduce human error risk
  • Established cross-functional review cadences keeping compliance and engineering in continuous alignment
  • Built operational resilience metrics into program health reviews — making resilience visible and measurable

The Outcome That Mattered

Compliance became a program asset rather than a tax. Teams understood their compliance posture in real time, automation reduced the burden, and resilience became something we could measure, discuss, and continuously improve — not just hope for.
